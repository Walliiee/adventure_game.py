# Wildlands: Narrative Design (Project Memory)

**Purpose:** This is the story that supports the full game. All content (training, skills, learning, NPCs, regions, exhibition) must align with this. Update this doc when we change the story.

---

## Premise (one sentence)

*You are 7. In three years you’ll be allowed to go out into the world, explore, and catch friends. Until then you train — and you must get good at everything, choose one primary skill and two supplementary ones, and prove you’re ready.*

---

## Game structure (two acts)

| Act | When | What |
|-----|------|------|
| **Act 1: Training** | Ages 7 → 10 | Character trains at home/school. Build all skills; choose **one primary** (e.g. strength) and **two supplementary** (e.g. agility, smarts). Improvement is **player-facing learning**: the player must know or learn things (questions, challenges). Can consult parents, mentors, school teachers, or pets — or go it alone. |
| **Act 2: Keeper** | Age 10+ | Allowed to go out. Current game loop: explore regions, catch creatures in orbs, train/play companions, win the Ridgecamp Exhibition. (Existing `game/` loop; Ridgecamp, Mira, Sol, Ari.) |

The training phase is **added before** the existing catch/explore loop. You play through 7→10, then at 10 you enter the current game.

---

## Character choice

- **Pick a character** at the start (who they are — name, personality, maybe look).
- **All characters start at ground zero.** No starting stats, no advantages. Everyone begins equal; progress is earned in the training phase.

---

## Protagonist (the child)

- **Who:** The player’s character — 7 at the start, becoming 10 by the end of Act 1.
- **Want:** To be ready: skilled enough and “graduated” so they can go out and catch friends.
- **Tone:** Young, eager, capable of learning; can seek help or work alone.

---

## Skills (training phase)

- **All skills** must be raised to a generally good level.
- **Focus:** Choose **one primary** and **two supplementary** (e.g. primary = strength, supplementary = agility and smarts). Other skill names TBD (e.g. strength, agility, smarts, spirit, …).
- **Ways to improve:** Many — study, practice, mini-challenges, etc.
- **Player-facing learning:** Getting better requires the **player** to actually learn or demonstrate knowledge (answer questions, solve problems, complete challenges). Not just “click to train”; the player has to engage.

---

## Support system (training phase)

The character can improve by:

- **Consulting others:** Parents, mentors, school teachers, or even pets. (Each can offer different kinds of help or content.)
- **Going alone:** They can also choose to work by themselves.

Implementation can offer choices like “study with the teacher” vs “practice alone” vs “ask Mom” — with different learning content or difficulty.

---

## Setting

- **Act 1:** Home, school, local area — wherever the child trains (to be defined in world design).
- **Act 2:** Ridgecamp and the Wildlands (existing): Sunmeadow, Old Ruins, Silver River, Storm Canyon, Moonwood. Frontier outpost + wild, slightly magical creatures.

---

## Goal

- **Act 1:** Reach age 10 ready — all skills at a good level, primary + two supplementary chosen and developed, “graduation” or permission to go out.
- **Act 2:** Win the Ridgecamp Exhibition (existing goal). Be recognized as a Keeper.

---

## Opposition / stakes

- **Act 1:** Time and readiness. The player must actually learn and improve; failure or slacking means not being ready at 10 (or failing a readiness check).
- **Act 2:** Same as before — Ari as rival, the wilds, limited orbs/health. Losing the exhibition means try again.

---

## How to use this

- **Training phase:** All mechanics (skills, primary/supplementary, learning challenges, parents/mentors/teachers/pets) must support this story. No alternate backstory (e.g. “you were already a Keeper”).
- **Character pick:** Purely who they are; everyone starts at ground zero.
- **Act 2 content:** When adding region or NPC content, keep Ridgecamp/Wildlands tone and the “you earned your way here” arc.

**Keep this doc updated when we change the story.** It is the single source of truth for narrative.
